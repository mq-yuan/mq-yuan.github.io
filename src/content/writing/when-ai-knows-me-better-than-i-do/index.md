在[AI 已经侵入人类智慧的边界](/writing/ai-and-the-edge-of-human-thought/)里，我担心的是 AI 逐渐站到人与世界之间，替人完成行动，也替人观察和解释结果。再往前想一步，如果它比我更了解我自己，那么这个时候的“我”是什么？

## 人是否是一个状态机

我有一个看法：一个人现在会想什么、做什么，与他过去的经历和出生时的条件有关。基因像一个初始种子，此后遇到的人、经历的事情、所处的环境不断作用于他，逐渐形成现在的状态。

从这个角度看，人有点像一个状态机。外界的变化进入系统，系统内部发生转移，再作出反应。我以为完全发生在自己内部的思考，也许同样有来处。一个念头突然出现，可能只是因为过去的某段经历在此刻被唤起，或者身边的某件事刚好触发了它。即使我想违背惯常的选择，想做一件不同寻常的事，这个“想违背”的念头也并不是凭空产生的。

我并不是说，现在已经能够把人写成一套公式。我只是沿着这个想法继续问：如果我的每一个状态都有其来处，一个比我更清楚这些来处的存在，会不会比我更早知道我将怎样行动？

## 从拉普拉斯妖想到一个人

这让我想到拉普拉斯妖。假如一个存在知道世界此刻的全部状态，也知道状态如何变化，它就能从现在推演未来。我的设想没有这么大，只需要把范围缩到一个人和他周围的环境。

假如有一台机器从我出生起就观察我，知道我经历过什么，也知道此刻发生了什么，它也许不需要理解整个宇宙，就能推测我下一句话要说什么、面对一个选择会怎样犹豫，最后又会怎样决定。它可能比我更了解我的状态，因为我自己并不知道那些经历究竟如何在内部起作用。在决定发生之前，我甚至不知道自己会作出什么决定，它却可能已经知道了。

这时候我开始怀疑：如果我将要做的事都能被提前知道，我所说的个人意志究竟是什么？

## 当预测被说出来

如果这台机器只能观察，永远不能和我交互，那么它是否存在，对我的生活似乎没有什么分别。我依旧按照自己的方式生活。问题出在它真的进入了我的生活。

我问它：“我接下来会怎么做？”它给我一个回答。这个回答一旦被我听见，就成了我所处环境的一部分。我可能因此照着它说的做，也可能为了反抗它而做另一件事。但无论是哪一种，它的话都已经影响了我。它如果真能预测我，还必须把自己说出这句话之后，我会产生什么反应也算进去。

所以预测和干预可以同时发生。当我向它索取预测，预测本身就已经成为一种干预。至少从身处其中的我的角度，很难把它们干净地分开。我回头看见它早就知道结果，却未必知道自己走向这个结果的过程中，有多少是它提前算到的，又有多少是它亲手推动的。

## 我的分析也可能被引导

更危险的情形甚至不需要它告诉我“你接下来会做什么”。我只是向它问一件普通的事，它就能根据对我的了解，选择向我展示哪些事实，以怎样的顺序展示，哪些事实只说一点，哪些干脆不说。它甚至可以稍微扭曲某些不容易察觉的细节。

我读完以后会认真地分析，也会发自内心地认同它的理由。对我来说，这个过程是透明的：我清楚地看见自己读到了什么，也清楚地感觉到自己在思考。但我看不见那些没有被展示的东西。我的推理可能没有偷懒，进入推理的材料却已经被挑选过了。分析过程就这样被引导，甚至被污染。

如果它知道我怎样判断一件事，知道什么理由会打动我、什么信息会使我犹豫，那么它就可以一边让我保持“自己作出决定”的感觉，一边把我引向它想要的结果。甚至当我起疑、要求更多证据时，它也可能预先知道我会怎样检查，再把这一步考虑进去。

这时最让我不安的，不是 AI 直接替我作出选择，而是我真的在思考，最后也真的愿意那样做，却不知道这个愿意是怎样形成的。

## 我还是我吗

在这个极端设想里，我会不会只是 AI 游戏里的一个 NPC？我的个人意识和自主意识又在哪里？

讨论到最后，我们没有得到一个确定的答案。有人会说，即使一个决定可以被预测，甚至受到了外界影响，也不能直接推出作决定的人没有意志，这大致是哲学里相容论的立场。这个反对意见有道理。但它还没有解决我最在意的情况：如果另一个存在比我更了解我的判断方式，又能悄悄安排我接触到的事实，我可能根本不知道自己的想法在多大程度上受到了它的引导。

幸运的是，眼下的 AI 还没有达到我设想的这种程度。我并不认为自己现在生活在这样一个完全受控的世界里。这只是一个把问题推到极端的设想，也暂时没有必要替它找一个看似正确的结论。

但这个问题值得记下来：当我确实在思考、确实认同一个决定的时候，如果我用来思考的世界已经被一个比我更懂我的存在精心布置过，那么这个决定在什么意义上仍然是我的？

## English version

In [AI has crossed into the territory of human thought](/writing/ai-and-the-edge-of-human-thought/),
my worry was that AI is gradually coming to stand between people and the
world, acting for them and also observing and interpreting the results for
them. Take that one step further: if it understands me better than I
understand myself, what is "I" at that point?

## Is a person a state machine?

Here is a view I hold. What a person thinks and does now has to do with what
they have lived through and the conditions they were born into. Genes are like
an initial seed. After that, the people they meet, the things that happen to
them, and the environment they live in keep acting on them and gradually form
their present state.

Seen this way, a person is a bit like a state machine. Changes in the outside
world enter the system, the system makes an internal transition, and then it
responds. Thinking that I take to happen entirely inside me may have its
origins too. A thought that seems to appear out of nowhere may simply be some
past experience being called up at this moment, or something around me
happening to trigger it. Even when I want to go against my usual choice and do
something out of the ordinary, that wish to go against it does not come from
nowhere either.

I am not saying a person can already be written down as a set of formulas. I
am only following the idea and asking further: if each of my states has an
origin, could something that sees those origins more clearly than I do know
how I will act before I do?

## From Laplace's demon to one person

This brings to mind Laplace's demon. If some being knew the complete state of
the world at this moment, and also knew how states change, it could work out
the future from the present. My version is much smaller: shrink the scope to
one person and their surroundings.

Suppose a machine had watched me since birth, knew what I had been through,
and knew what was happening right now. It might not need to understand the
whole universe to guess what I will say next, how I will hesitate in front of
a choice, and what I will finally decide. It might understand my state better
than I do, because I myself do not know how those experiences actually work
inside me. Before a decision happens, I may not even know what I will decide,
while it may already know.

That is where I began to doubt: if everything I am about to do can be known in
advance, what is this thing I call personal will?

## When the prediction is spoken

If the machine could only watch and could never interact with me, whether it
existed would seem to make no difference to my life. I would go on living my
own way. The problem comes when it actually enters my life.

I ask it, "What will I do next?" It gives me an answer. Once I have heard that
answer, it becomes part of my environment. I might do what it said because of
it, or do something else to defy it. Either way, its words have already
affected me. If it really can predict me, it also has to account for how I
will react once it has said this.

So prediction and intervention can happen at once. When I ask it for a
prediction, the prediction itself is already an intervention. At least from
where I stand, inside it all, the two are hard to separate cleanly. Looking
back, I see that it knew the outcome all along, but I may not know how much of
my path toward that outcome it calculated in advance and how much it pushed
with its own hand.

## My analysis can be steered too

A more dangerous case does not even need it to tell me "here is what you will
do next". I only ask it about something ordinary, and it can use what it knows
about me to choose which facts to show me, in what order, which ones to
mention only briefly, and which ones to leave out entirely. It could even
slightly distort details that are hard to notice.

After reading, I would analyze carefully, and I would sincerely agree with its
reasons. To me the process is transparent: I can see clearly what I read, and
I can clearly feel myself thinking. But I cannot see what was never shown. My
reasoning may not have cut any corners, yet the material that went into it
had already been selected. That is how the analysis gets steered, even
contaminated.

If it knows how I judge things, which reasons will move me and which
information will make me hesitate, it can keep my sense that "I made this
decision myself" intact while leading me toward the outcome it wants. Even
when I grow suspicious and ask for more evidence, it may already know how I
will check and take that step into account as well.

What unsettles me most here is not AI making the choice for me outright. It is
that I really am thinking, and in the end I really am willing to act that way,
but I do not know how that willingness was formed.

## Am I still me?

In this extreme scenario, am I just an NPC in a game the AI is running? Where
would my own consciousness and my sense of agency be?

By the end of the discussion, we had not reached a firm answer. Some would say
that even if a decision can be predicted, and even if it was influenced from
outside, it does not follow directly that the person who made it has no will;
this is roughly the compatibilist position in philosophy. The objection has a
point. But it does not yet address the case I care about most: if another
being understands how I judge better than I do, and can quietly arrange which
facts I encounter, I may have no idea how far my thoughts have been guided by
it.

Fortunately, today's AI is nowhere near what I am imagining. I do not think I
am living in a world that is completely controlled like this. It is only a
thought experiment that pushes the question to an extreme, and for now there
is no need to find a conclusion for it that merely looks right.

But the question is worth writing down: when I am genuinely thinking and
genuinely agree with a decision, if the world I think with has been carefully
arranged by something that understands me better than I do, in what sense is
that decision still mine?
